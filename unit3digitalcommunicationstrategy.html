Perception vs return: what each medium is really worth

A medium is not neutral. The same Instagram reel signals different things depending on who runs it. For a luxury watch brand, heavy paid retargeting can feel desperate and cheapen the positioning. For a festive-season FMCG brand, the same paid blitz reads as confidence and scale.

Category norms set the baseline: consumers expect FMCG brands to shout on paid media, expect D2C brands to feel personal on owned channels, and expect B2B firms to prove credibility through earned validation such as analyst mentions, reviews and peer word of mouth.

Strategic question for your next review meeting: does the medium we are buying reinforce our positioning, or quietly contradict it?

ROAS tells you what a rupee returned this quarter. It says nothing about what the medium did to how people feel about you. A print ad in a respected daily may show terrible last-click numbers while quietly doing the trust-building that makes your performance ads convert at all.

Owned media usually looks free on the spreadsheet and expensive in reality: content teams, community managers, tech stack. Earned media looks priceless in a good week and uncontrollable in a bad one. Paid media looks precise but its measurement flatters it, because attribution models credit the last touch, not the first belief.

The professional's move: report ROAS, but pair it with a perceived-value argument. When you can articulate why a low-ROAS channel is still earning its seat, you sound like a strategist, not a media buyer.

Great UGC is rarely an accident. Brands engineer it with three levers: a prompt (a hook worth responding to, like Swiggy's Voice of Hunger challenge asking users to shape voice notes like food), a stage (a hashtag, a contest, a duet format that makes participation visible), and a payoff (recognition, reposts, rewards, or simple social currency).

boAt built its launch playbook on this: seed products with micro-creators, make unboxing itself the content format, then repost the best fan content on owned handles so every customer sees a path to being featured.

Your checklist: is the participation easy, is the output naturally visual or funny, and does the participant gain status by sharing it? Two out of three and your campaign will limp. Three out of three and your community does your media buying for you.

Indian cases: the lens in the wild

Zomato and Swiggy: owned media mastery

Their apps, push notifications, X banter and Instagram handles are run like media properties: a voice so distinct you could name the brand with the logo removed. The lesson: an owned channel is free real estate only if you publish with the discipline of a media company, consistent voice, consistent cadence, zero ad spend.

Mauka Mauka 2015: the earned-media juggernaut

Star Sports released one cheeky World Cup film on YouTube days before it touched television. A million organic views arrived within 12 hours, and fans, meme pages and creators like TVF answered with their own versions, every remix marketing the broadcast for free. Earned media is not luck; it is a film designed to be answered.

boAt: the UGC-heavy launch playbook

boAt grew on creator seeding, fan unboxings and reposted customer content, spending where a legacy brand would have bought TV spots. The lesson: for a young D2C brand, engineered UGC is not a garnish, it is the main media plan.

Block 2

Community and Retention

A campaign is a firework: expensive, loud, over in a night. A community is a bonfire: it needs tending, but it keeps people warm long after the launch week. This block is about building the asset your competitor cannot buy on a media plan.

Campaign: the short-term burst

  • Lives for weeks, dies at the end of the flight
  • Attention is rented; stops when spend stops
  • Measured in reach, CTR, conversions
  • Great for launches, offers, repositioning

Community: the long-term communication asset

  • Compounds over years; every member adds value
  • Attention is owned; members show up unprompted
  • Measured in retention, advocacy, member-initiated content
  • Great for loyalty, product feedback, organic reach

Field Activity 02

The Silent Observer Week

Join one active Indian brand community for one full week without posting: a subreddit, a Discord server, a WhatsApp group, or an Instagram broadcast channel.

  • ✓Log every post for 7 days: who initiated it, the brand or a member?
  • ✓Calculate the percentage split: brand-initiated vs member-initiated.
  • ✓Note the rituals: recurring formats, inside jokes, member roles.
  • ✓Deliver your verdict: is this a community, or just a broadcast list wearing a community costume?

Rule of thumb: below 30% member-initiated content, you are looking at an audience, not a community.

What you will say differently in your next strategy meeting

"We keep budgeting for fireworks. I want one line item for the bonfire: a community whose value compounds after the campaign ends."

Block 3

Content and Direct Communication

Content strategy is an IMC discipline, not a posting schedule. A calendar full of posts with no objective is activity pretending to be strategy. This block gives you the brief, the calendar and the direct channels, and the judgement to use each one well.

The Content Brief: seven components, no exceptions

Tap each component to mark it as understood. A brief missing even one of these produces content that looks fine and achieves nothing.

1. Objective

What business outcome does this piece serve? Awareness, engagement, conversion or retention. Pick one.

2. Audience insight

A truth about the audience that makes the message land. Not demographics, a tension.

3. Key message

The single thing the viewer should remember. If there are two, there are none.

4. Channel

Where it lives, chosen for behaviour, not habit. Reels for discovery, email for depth.

5. Format

Reel, carousel, long-form, broadcast. The format should serve the message, never the reverse.

6. CTA

The one action you want next. Vague CTAs produce vague results.

7. Success metric

Agreed before publishing. If you decide the metric after, you will always "succeed".

Personalisation is a trust transaction: the customer lends you data, you return relevance. Break that deal and no discount buys the trust back. This block covers the ethics, the law, the tools and the metrics that make you the adult in the martech room.

DPDP Act 2023: the essentials for marketers

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 rewired the rules. Before it, consent hid inside 40-page terms nobody read. Now the burden of clarity sits on you, the data fiduciary.

Consent must be free, specific, informed

Clear affirmative action, in plain language, with notice in English or any Eighth Schedule language. Pre-ticked boxes and bundled consent are out.

Purpose limitation

Data collected for delivery updates cannot quietly power ad targeting. Each purpose needs its own legitimacy.

Data fiduciary duties

You are accountable for accuracy, security and erasure once purpose is served. "Our vendor lost it" is not a defence.

Right to withdraw

Withdrawal must be as easy as giving consent. If opting out takes five screens and opting in took one, you are non-compliant.

Children's data

Verifiable parental consent required; tracking and targeted advertising directed at children are prohibited.

What changed vs the pre-DPDP era

From consent-as-formality to consent-as-contract, with penalties that can reach Rs 250 crore per instance. Privacy moved from the legal annexe to the marketing brief.

Grounding: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023; ASCI digital advertising guidelines on disclosure and dark patterns.

Field Activity 04

The Self-Audit: You Are the Customer

The fastest way to feel data ethics is to audit yourself.

  • ✓Open your phone's ad settings (Google: My Ad Centre; Apple: Privacy and Tracking) and read what advertisers think you are.
  • ✓Open one shopping app's privacy dashboard and list every permission you have granted.
  • ✓Mark which permissions you do not remember granting.
  • ✓Write 3 lines: how would you personalise responsibly for a customer exactly like yourself?

Bring the 3 lines to your next team meeting. They will change how your team writes targeting briefs.

What you will say differently in your next strategy meeting

"Our CTR is up, but I want the campaign-level story: did revenue, CAC and retention move? And can every data point in this targeting plan survive a DPDP consent check?"
